## What you're building

A clinic or salon books appointments. **Customers** book and cancel in a phone app. **Staff** see and reschedule their own day. **Owners** manage staff, hours and pricing from a web dashboard. Reminders go out the night before, payments are taken on booking, and none of it may leak between clinics.

## Why this project, at this point

Vault had one user and one trust boundary. Book-a-Slot is the first product with **genuine organisational complexity**, and it is where every Architecture A3 lesson stops being abstract:

| A3 lesson | Where it lands here |
|---|---|
| Tenants and isolation | Clinic A must never see Clinic B, enforced below the query |
| Roles and permissions | Three roles whose rules depend on the relationship, not just the role |
| Background jobs | Reminders, which must not send twice |
| Caches | The day view, which must never show a slot as free when it is not |
| Environments | Two frontends and an API, promoted through staging by a pipeline |

It is also the first project with **enough independent surfaces to justify a real parallel crew** — backend, app and dashboard, from one contract.

## The stack and why

**Flutter** for the customer app, as before. **A web framework** for the admin dashboard, because dense tables, filters and forms are where web libraries and component registries are strongest — and because building one product across two frontends from one contract is the actual lesson. **FastAPI and PostgreSQL**, **Redis** for jobs and rate limits, **payments in test mode**, and a CI pipeline that deploys.

## Complexity, by strand

- **Architecture** — three roles, background jobs, two clients generated from one OpenAPI file.
- **Security** — an authorisation test for every endpoint and role, webhook signature verification, rate limits, and an audit log of who changed what.
- **UI & UX** — one design system shared by app and dashboard. Booking in three taps; a dashboard that works on a tablet at the front desk.
- **Product** — onboarding, reminders, cancellations, and a no-show policy that someone has to decide.
- **Motion** — calendar transitions and slot selection, where animation carries meaning rather than decoration.

## How you work

Orchestrator and workers, with a pipeline gate. The architect owns the contract. Backend, app and dashboard builders work in three worktrees; the gate then runs code review, security review and QA across both frontends.

## Done when

- The crew run from lesson 4.11 works for a real feature, at this size.
- Merges to main deploy to staging automatically, and end-to-end flows pass on both frontends.
- A cross-tenant test exists for every endpoint and all of them pass.
- Double-booking the same slot is impossible, and you have a test that proves it under concurrency.
