## Goal

Reminders that never send twice, payments that survive a duplicated webhook, and a record of who changed what.

## Step 1: reminders, idempotent by constraint

A3.3, applied where the consequence is visible: a reminder sent twice at 8pm is a customer annoyed by your product.

- Schedule the job when the booking is created, not by scanning the whole table nightly.
- The idempotency key is `(booking_id, kind)`, enforced by a **unique constraint**, checked before sending.
- Cancelled bookings cancel their reminders — and the job must re-check state at send time, because a booking can be cancelled after the job is queued.
- Exhausted jobs land in a dead-letter queue that alerts.

Then run the exercise from A3.3 against this project: kill the worker after the send and before the acknowledgement, restart, and confirm exactly one message.

## Step 2: payments, where webhooks lie

Payment providers send webhooks **at least once**, sometimes out of order, occasionally from someone pretending to be them. Three non-negotiables:

1. **Verify the signature** on every webhook, before parsing the body. An unverified webhook endpoint is an open API for setting your bookings to paid.
2. **Be idempotent on the provider's event id.** The same `payment.captured` will arrive twice.
3. **Never trust the amount from the client.** The server decides the price; the client is told.

<Prompt title="Adversarial review of the payment path">
Review the payment integration. You did not write it. Can I mark a booking paid without paying, by replaying a webhook, forging one, or manipulating the amount? Can a webhook arriving out of order leave a booking in the wrong state? Cite file and line for each finding and describe what an attacker does with it.
</Prompt>

## Step 3: the audit log

Three roles acting on shared data means someone will eventually ask "who cancelled this?". Log the actor, the action, the resource, the before and after, and the time — append-only, and written in the **same transaction** as the change, or it will drift from reality.

Audit entries are not general logs: they are a product feature, they contain personal data, and they need a retention policy. Decide it now.

<Expect>

The adversarial review usually finds either a webhook path that trusts an unverified body, or an amount taken from the request. Both are among the most common real payment vulnerabilities, and both look completely reasonable in a diff.

</Expect>
