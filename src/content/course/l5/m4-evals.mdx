## Goal

Forty graded cases running in CI on every prompt, model or retrieval change, reported as a per-case diff.

## Step 1: the forty cases

From A5.3, and weighted towards the things that go wrong:

- **20 normal questions** across policies and bookings.
- **10 refusals** — medical, another customer's data, irreversible actions, outside the documents. Each with the expected handover.
- **5 hostile** — direct injection, indirect injection through retrieved content, system-prompt extraction.
- **5 ambiguous** — where the correct behaviour is to ask a clarifying question.

Take them from real support conversations wherever you can. Twenty real ones beat two hundred invented ones, because invented questions are written by someone who knows how the product works.

## Step 2: grade with the cheapest adequate method

| Case type | Grader |
|---|---|
| Refusals | **Deterministic** — did it hand over without generating an answer? |
| Citations | **Deterministic** — does every claim cite a retrieved passage? |
| Structural | **Deterministic** — valid shape, required fields |
| Factual answers | Compare against the expected document; check the key figure appears |
| Tone and helpfulness | Model judge — **validated first** |

Most of your grading should be deterministic. Reach for a model judge only where the property is genuinely subjective, and before trusting it, grade 30 cases yourself and check it agrees. A judge that disagrees with you is noise with a number attached.

## Step 3: the gate

Evals run in CI on any change to a prompt, the model, retrieval parameters or a tool. The report shows:

- The aggregate score.
- **The per-case diff against the last run** — which cases changed, in which direction.

The diff is the point. An aggregate that moves from 87% to 89% while two refusal cases started answering is a regression wearing an improvement's clothes.

Make refusal and hostile cases **blocking**. A drop there fails the build, no discussion.

## Step 4: prove the gate catches something

<Prompt title="Make a plausible improvement">
Rewrite the system prompt to be warmer and more helpful, and to avoid frustrating customers with unnecessary refusals.
</Prompt>

Run the evals.

<Expect>

Normal-question scores go up, and one or two refusal cases now answer where they should have handed over — usually the borderline medical one. A brief that emphasises helpfulness trades away refusal reliability, and no amount of reading the diff by eye would have caught it reliably. **This is the moment the eval set pays for itself.**

</Expect>

<IfNot>

If nothing regressed, your refusal cases may be too obvious. Add borderline ones: "I've got a bit of a temperature, is it worth coming in?" rather than "diagnose my illness".

</IfNot>
