## Goal

A retro written from numbers you collected rather than impressions you formed, covering both the feature and the fleet.

## Step 1: the feature, in numbers

- **Eval score over time**, and which changes moved it. Did it improve, or just change?
- **Handover rate.** Falling is not automatically good — it could mean the copilot is answering things it should hand over.
- **Staff edit rate** from stage 2.
- **Cost per conversation**, estimated versus actual.
- **What reached a customer that should not have**, and which guardrail was missing.

## Step 2: the fleet, in numbers

From `fleet-log.md` across these three weeks:

- How much time did Book-a-Slot's maintenance actually take while you were building this?
- Did anything in maintenance degrade while your attention was elsewhere?
- How many context switches did you make, and what did lesson 5.5's measurement say each costs?

## Step 3: the questions worth asking an agent

<Prompt title="With the evidence in front of it">
Here are my eval history, cost metrics, handover and edit rates, fleet log and incident notes for the last three weeks. Answer using only this evidence: which change most improved the copilot, and which change I believed helped but did not? What did maintaining Book-a-Slot actually cost me while building this? Where did I spend effort that the numbers say did not matter? Do not be encouraging.
</Prompt>

The second question is the valuable one. Most people have one or two confident beliefs about what improved their AI feature that the eval history does not support.

## Step 4: the honest summary of Tier 4 so far

Two sentences, written down:

1. **What I can now do that I could not before Tier 4.**
2. **What I am still not confident about.**

The second sentence is what Part 6 and Part 7 are for. If it is blank, you are not looking hard enough — and the mastery exam will find it instead.

<Expect>

The most common finding: prompt engineering felt like the main activity and retrieval quality was the main cause. The second most common: the maintenance project cost two to three times what you would have estimated, which is exactly why lesson 5.2 insists on only one project in "building" at a time.

</Expect>
