## Goal

The copilot live behind a flag, rolled out gradually, inside budget — and Book-a-Slot no worse than before.

## Step 1: the flag, and why it is not optional

An AI feature is the strongest case for a feature flag there is. Quality problems appear only with real users and real questions, and the ability to turn it off **without a deploy** is the difference between a bad morning and a bad week.

Flag it per clinic, so you can enable one friendly clinic first.

## Step 2: roll out in stages, with a stop rule for each

| Stage | Who | Watch | Stop if |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Internal only | Every conversation, read by you | Anything on the refusal list is answered |
| 2 | One clinic, staff-facing — drafts a reply, staff send it | Staff edit rate | Staff rewrite more than half |
| 3 | One clinic, customer-facing | Handover rate, complaints, cost | Handover rate climbs, or any data leak |
| 4 | All clinics | The dashboard | Budget or quality alert |

Stage 2 is the one worth insisting on. Staff-facing drafting gives you real questions and real quality signal with a human between the model and the customer — and the **edit rate is the most honest quality metric you will get**, better than any eval score, because it is people voting with their time.

## Step 3: watch two products at once

This is a Part 5 milestone as much as an A5 one. You are changing a live product, so watch both:

**The new feature:** cost per conversation, p95 latency, handover rate, fallback rate, refusal rate.

**Book-a-Slot itself:** its existing alerts, its p99, its error rate. A new feature that quietly slows the booking API is a regression in a product that was working.

Add to `fleet-log.md` daily during rollout.

<Callout type="warn" label="Cost behaves differently with real users">
  <p>Your evals asked 40 tidy questions once. Real users ask long rambling ones, paste screenshots-worth of text, and follow up five times. Expect cost per conversation to exceed your estimate at stage 3, and have the per-user daily ceiling already in code rather than reaching for it afterwards.</p>
</Callout>

<Exercise id="l5m5-1" title="Roll out, and let a stop rule stop you" time="over a week" predict="Which stage will you actually pause at?">

1. Flag the feature per clinic.
2. Run stage 1 for two days, reading every conversation yourself.
3. Stage 2 for three days. Record the staff edit rate.
4. Stage 3 for one clinic, watching cost and handover rate daily.
5. If a stop rule fires, **stop**. Fix, then resume from the previous stage.

</Exercise>

<Expect>

Most people stop at stage 2 or 3 at least once, usually on cost or on a refusal case that reality produced and the eval set did not. Every real stop becomes a new eval case — that is the loop that makes the product improve rather than drift.

</Expect>
