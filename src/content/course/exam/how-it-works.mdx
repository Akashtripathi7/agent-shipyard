<Analogy>
  <p>A driving test, not a written one. Nobody asks you to recite the rules. They put you on an unfamiliar road and watch what you do at the junction.</p>
</Analogy>

## What is being tested

Not recall. Every fact in this course is on the reference pages, and looking things up is what professionals do.

What is being tested is the Tier 4 gate:

> **You can defend a workflow and an architecture choice to someone who disagrees with you.**

Three things have to be true at once. You must **choose** deliberately, **know why** in terms of what you are optimising for, and **know what would make you wrong**.

## How each scenario works

Each gives you a product, a team, constraints and a deadline. For each, produce:

1. **The architecture** — components, data, boundaries. Which architecture parts apply, and which you are explicitly ruling out.
2. **The workflow** — your rung, your crew, your gates, your depths per area.
3. **The three decisions you would lose sleep over**, each with a falsifier.
4. **What you would build in week one**, specifically.
5. **The strongest argument against your own design.**

Item five is not a formality. A design you cannot argue against is one you have not understood.

## How to grade yourself

Write your answer first. Then, for each scenario, run this:

<Prompt title="Adversarial examination, fresh session, different model if possible">
Here is a scenario and my proposed architecture and workflow. You are examining this work and you are not inclined to be kind. For each of my decisions: what am I assuming without evidence, what breaks first, and what would a more experienced engineer have done differently? Name any place I applied a pattern that this situation does not justify, and any place I under-engineered something that will hurt. Then give me the three questions I should have asked about this scenario and did not. Do not be agreeable and do not give me a score.
</Prompt>

Then judge yourself against four marks:

| | You are there when |
|---|---|
| **Choosing** | Every significant decision was made on purpose, not by default or by habit |
| **Reasoning** | You can state what you optimised for, and in what order |
| **Falsifiability** | Each load-bearing decision has an observation that would prove it wrong |
| **Restraint** | You ruled things out. A design that applies every part of this course has failed |

**Restraint is the one most people miss.** Reaching for services, queues, crews and caches because you have been taught them is the failure mode this exam is built to catch. A scenario with eleven users and a two-week deadline should produce a small, dull, correct design.

## A note on the answers

There are no answer keys, and that is deliberate. Each scenario is written so that at least two defensible designs exist, and the difference between a strong answer and a weak one is never the diagram — it is whether the reasoning survives the examination.

If you want a second opinion, take a scenario to someone who has built this kind of product and argue about it. That is what the gate actually describes.
