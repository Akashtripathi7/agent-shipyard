## What makes this different

Every project so far came with a stack, a shape and a crew. This one comes with nothing. **You choose the product, design the architecture, and run the workflow you wrote in Part 7** — and the only measure of success is evidence you collected yourself.

That is the point. Tiers 1 to 3 taught you to execute. Tier 4 is about judgment, and judgment cannot be assessed on a problem where someone has already told you the answer.

## Choosing your product

The constraints matter more than the idea:

- **Real enough to have consequences.** Someone other than you should want it to work — even one person.
- **Small enough to ship in four weeks** alongside everything else alive in your fleet.
- **At least two of these**, or you will not exercise Tier 4: more than one user role, data that must not leak between users, a background job, an AI feature, an offline requirement, or a second client.
- **Something you will keep running afterwards.** Maintenance is where most of what you learned actually lands.

<Callout type="warn" label="The most common capstone failure">
  <p>Choosing something too big, then spending four weeks on architecture that never meets a user. <b>A small shipped product teaches more than a large unshipped one</b> — and the whole course has been arguing that. If you cannot describe it in two sentences, it is too big.</p>
</Callout>

## What you must produce, beyond the product

- **A workflow design**, written before you build, and a record of every time you deviated from it.
- **Two numbers**, weekly: interventions per feature and escapes.
- **Architecture decisions** written as ADRs, each with a falsifier.
- **A shipped, running product**, with alerts that reach you and a runbook.
- **A month review** grounded in the numbers rather than in how it felt.

## How this is assessed

By you, honestly, against the Tier 4 gate:

> *You can defend a workflow and an architecture choice to someone who disagrees with you.*

The mastery exam that follows will test exactly that on problems you have not seen. The capstone is where you earn it.

## Done when

- The product is live and someone other than you has used it.
- Four weeks of metrics exist, and you can explain what moved them.
- Every load-bearing decision has an ADR with a falsifier.
- You have deviated from your own workflow at least once and written down why.
- You can state one thing about your workflow that the evidence proved wrong.
