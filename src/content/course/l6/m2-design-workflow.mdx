## Goal

A project-specific workflow, decided in advance, with the deviations you expect and the evidence you will collect.

## Step 1: which rung, and why

From lesson 6.2, with the pain named in the past tense. For a solo four-week capstone the honest answer is usually **rung 2**, climbing to 3 only if two genuinely independent surfaces appear.

Write what would make you climb, and what would make you climb back down.

## Step 2: the crew, if any

Seven questions, answered for this product:

1. Are there genuinely independent surfaces? (If no, you have one builder.)
2. Does anything need a reviewer that cannot edit?
3. Does anything need a different model reviewing than building?
4. Is any work read-heavy enough to belong in a subagent?
5. What must only you decide?
6. What is the blast radius of the worst thing an agent could do here?
7. What does each role's tool list need to be, at minimum?

Question 5 is the one that pays. For most capstones the list is: the data model, anything touching personal data, and any irreversible decision.

## Step 3: depths per area

Not every part of one product deserves the same care. From your WORKFLOW.md:

| Area | Depth | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Auth, personal data, payments | GA | Irreversible or dangerous |
| Core product flow | Beta | Users depend on it |
| Admin tools you alone use | Alpha | You are the only victim |
| Spikes | Prototype | Being deleted |

Writing this table now stops the two failures: treating everything as GA and shipping nothing, or treating everything as Alpha and shipping something that leaks.

## Step 4: the gates, and what is law

What runs on every commit, what blocks a merge, and what you would be upset to discover was skipped. Keep it small — a four-week solo project with ten gates is a project about gates.

## Step 5: commit to the measurement

Create `docs/metrics.md` now, with the two columns from lesson 7.2. Deciding to measure after you notice a problem is how you end up with no baseline.

<Callout type="tip" label="Expect to deviate, and record it">
  <p>You will break your own workflow at some point — skipping review at 11pm, merging without the contract test. <b>Write down every deviation and why.</b> That log is the most honest input to the month review, and far more useful than a workflow you claim to have followed perfectly.</p>
</Callout>
