## Goal

A two-sentence product description, an explicit out-of-scope list, and the constraints that will shape everything after.

## Step 1: write it in two sentences

If you cannot, it is not one product yet. Two sentences: who it is for, and what it lets them do that they cannot do now.

## Step 2: the out-of-scope list, which is longer

This is the scope stage from Part 2, applied to a whole product. Write down what v1 explicitly does **not** do, and why. Expect it to be three times the length of the feature list.

<Prompt title="Adversarial scoping, fresh session">
Here is my product in two sentences. Challenge it. What is genuinely needed for a v1 that a real person would use, and what am I including out of enthusiasm? What am I assuming about my users that I have not checked? What is the smallest version that still delivers the core promise? Do not be agreeable, and do not suggest additions.
</Prompt>

The last clause matters. Ask an agent about a product idea without it and you will get features.

## Step 3: name the two Tier 4 elements

Which two from the overview list will this exercise, and where? Write them down — they are the reason this capstone teaches anything beyond Tier 2.

## Step 4: the constraints that decide the architecture

Before any design, write the numbers and facts that will drive it:

- **Users** — how many in month one, honestly. Usually a single-digit number, and that should change your architecture.
- **Data** — what is sensitive, what must never leak, what must be deletable.
- **Availability** — what happens if it is down for an hour? For most capstones, the honest answer is "nothing much", and that is permission to keep it simple.
- **Your time** — hours per week, alongside your fleet.
- **Money** — what you are willing to spend monthly.

<Expect>

The adversarial scoping usually removes a third of what you planned. The constraints section is where most people realise they were about to build for an imagined scale — writing "8 users in month one" next to a plan for a message queue is a clarifying moment.

</Expect>
