## Goal

A working product, built through your own loop, with three weeks of honest metrics and a deviations log.

## The weekly rhythm

**Monday** — pick the week's slices, assign a depth to each from your M2 table, write the briefs.

**Through the week** — run the loop. One line in the metrics file per intervention. One line in the deviations log every time you skip your own process.

**Friday, ten minutes** — fill in the two numbers, and one sentence on what caused most of the interventions.

That Friday ritual is the whole measurement system. It survives because it is small.

## What to watch for in yourself

Four patterns that show up in nearly every capstone:

1. **Depth inflation.** Everything drifts towards GA because it feels responsible. Check your M2 table weekly — an admin tool you alone use does not need a second-model review.
2. **Silent rung climbing.** You add a subagent, then a gate, then a second worktree, none of it decided. Each addition should be a sentence in the deviations log.
3. **Skipping the stage that is tedious.** Usually verification. Note it rather than fixing it immediately — whether you skip it three weeks running is the data.
4. **Building for the user count you wish you had.** Reread your M1 constraints when you feel the urge to add a queue.

## Using the fleet discipline

This product is now in your fleet, and your other projects have not stopped. Apply Part 5:

- One project in **building** — this one. Everything else in maintenance or dormant.
- The daily fleet log, one line per project.
- A stopping point and a handoff line before you switch away.

If the capstone drags on past four weeks, the usual cause is not the product. It is another project quietly still in *building*.

<Callout type="tip" label="Ship something in week one">
  <p>Deploy the smallest possible version — even one endpoint and one screen — in the first week, behind a flag if you like. A product that has never been deployed hides an entire class of problems until the moment you have least time for them.</p>
</Callout>

<Exercise id="l6m4-1" title="The Friday ten minutes" time="10 minutes, weekly" predict="Which of the four patterns will you catch yourself in first?">

1. Fill in interventions and escapes for the week.
2. Sort the week's interventions by cause. Name the biggest.
3. Read your deviations log. Was each deviation a good call or a tired one?
4. Check your depth table — did anything drift?
5. Write one sentence about what to change next week. Change **one** thing.

</Exercise>

<Expect>

Depth inflation is the most common, and the intervention log is almost always dominated by underspecified briefs — the same finding as every previous project, which by now should be persuasive rather than surprising.

</Expect>
