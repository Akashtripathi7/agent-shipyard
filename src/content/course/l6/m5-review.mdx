## Goal

A live product with someone else using it, a runbook, and a review that tells you something you did not already believe.

## Step 1: ship it properly

- Deployed through a pipeline, promoted from staging.
- Alerts on user-visible symptoms, each with a runbook line.
- A rollback you have practised and timed.
- Deletion and export working, if you hold personal data.
- A budget alert on the account.

## Step 2: a real person uses it

Not you. Watch them if you can, and say nothing while they struggle — the silence is where the learning is.

Write down every place they hesitated. Hesitation is the cheapest usability signal there is, and agents cannot generate it for you.

## Step 3: operate it for a week

A week in maintenance, with lesson 5.2's returning test: **can you ship a small fix using only what is written down?** Whatever you had to remember is a gap in the runbook.

## Step 4: the month review

With every number in front of you:

<Prompt title="The review that is worth having">
Here are four weeks of metrics, my deviations log, my ADRs, my gate findings, my fleet log and the issues found after shipping. Using only this evidence:
1. What caused most of my interventions, and did it change over the month?
2. Which of my workflow rules never caught anything?
3. Which deviation from my own process was the right call, and which was tiredness?
4. Which architecture decision does the evidence say I got wrong?
5. What am I doing out of habit that the numbers say does not matter?
Do not be encouraging. I want the pattern.
</Prompt>

Then answer, in your own words and in writing:

- **One thing my workflow does that the evidence says is unnecessary.** Remove it.
- **One thing it is missing.** Add it.
- **One belief about how I work that turned out to be wrong.**

That third one is the capstone's real output. If you cannot fill it in, you have not looked hard enough at the log.

## Step 5: the Tier 4 gate

> *You can defend a workflow and an architecture choice to someone who disagrees with you.*

Test it. Take your most significant architecture decision and your most significant workflow rule, and have a fresh session argue against both as hard as it can. Then defend them — out loud, or in writing, with the evidence.

If your defence is "it is best practice" or "it felt right", you have not cleared the gate yet. If it is "I optimised for X over Y because of this constraint, here is what would change my mind, and here is the measurement", you have.

The mastery exam is the same test on problems you have not seen.
