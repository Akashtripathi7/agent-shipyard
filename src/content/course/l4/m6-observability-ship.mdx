## Goal

A trace that finds a slow span across services in under a minute, alerts a person would act on, and an honest answer to whether you have cleared Tier 3.

## Step 1: tracing, and proving propagation

Add tracing across both services with the request id propagated. Then run A4.6's exercise on your own system: add 400ms of artificial latency deep inside one service, and find it **using the trace alone**.

The failure to watch for is a trace showing one span with no children — that means context is not being forwarded on outbound calls, which makes the whole thing decorative. Verify propagation before you trust it.

## Step 2: alerts a person would act on

For Courier Live, alert on symptoms a user would notice:

| Alert | Why it is a symptom, not a cause |
|---|---|
| Ingestion error rate above 1% for 5 minutes | Positions are being lost |
| p99 device-to-map latency above the budget | The map is visibly behind |
| Consumer lag growing without recovery | Events are backing up; the board is stale |
| Dead-letter queue non-empty | Something failed permanently and nobody knows |
| Saga records pending beyond a timeout | The leak from M5, caught early |

Not on that list: CPU at 80%. That is a cause, it is often fine, and it wakes someone for a number.

Every alert gets a **runbook line**: what it means, what to check first, what to do.

## Step 3: ship, then break it on purpose

Deploy through staging. Then, on staging under load, run the failures you have designed for:

1. Kill one ingestion instance. Does the system stay up and rebalance?
2. Stop the event bus for 60 seconds. Do consumers catch up without losing events?
3. Take the notification service down entirely. **Do deliveries still work?** This is the "fails alone" claim from M1 — if bookings break, your split did not buy what the ADR said it did.
4. Fill the dead-letter queue. Does the alert fire?

<Expect>

Number three is the honest test of the whole architecture. Many splits that look right on a diagram turn out to be synchronously coupled in one forgotten call, and the blast radius the ADR promised does not exist.

</Expect>

## Step 4: the Tier 3 gate, answered

> *A crew run finished without you reading every diff, because the gates caught what you would have caught.*

Across L2, L3 and L4 you have `docs/crew-log.md`, gate findings, CI history and post-release issues. Answer with numbers:

<Prompt title="The honest gate check">
Using only my crew logs, gate findings, CI history and post-release issues across Vault, Book-a-Slot and Courier Live: is my intervention rate per feature falling? Which failure mode from lesson 4.11 still dominates? What reached production that neither my gates nor I caught, and which single gate would have caught the most of it? Do not be encouraging — I want the pattern, not a summary.
</Prompt>

If the answer is that you are still reading every diff, that is useful information and not a failure. It usually means one specific gate is missing, and the question names it.
