## Goal

Three clients showing live position, each reconnecting cleanly, none of them ever quietly showing a stale courier as live.

## Step 1: pick the transport per client, not per product

A4.5's table, applied three times — and the answers differ:

| Client | Needs | Transport |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer tracking** | One courier, one direction, seconds matter | **SSE** |
| **Dispatcher board** | Many couriers, one direction, dense | **SSE**, one stream, server-filtered |
| **Courier app** | Sends constantly, receives assignments | **Polling for assignments**, plain HTTP posts for position |

The courier app is the interesting one. It is tempting to give it a WebSocket because it is "the most real-time" client, but it mostly *sends*, and sending is what plain HTTP is for. Assignments arrive rarely, so polling every 10 seconds is simpler, survives network changes, and costs almost nothing.

## Step 2: the honesty rules

A stale map that looks live is worse than a map that says it is stale. Three rules, all visible in the interface:

1. **Every position carries its own timestamp**, and the client shows age once it exceeds a threshold — "last seen 3 minutes ago" rather than a confident dot.
2. **The client ignores anything older than what it has already applied.** Out-of-order arrival is normal (A4.2).
3. **Disconnection is visible.** Not an error banner, but the map must not imply liveness it does not have.

## Step 3: survive the tunnel

The test that matters, from A4.5:

1. Stream position to the customer map.
2. Kill the network for two minutes.
3. Restore it.

The client must reconnect with backoff, catch up via `Last-Event-ID`, and — because two minutes may exceed the replay buffer — be corrected by the **periodic full snapshot**. During the outage it must show the position as ageing, not as current.

<Expect>

The first attempt reconnects and shows a two-minute-old position as though it were live, with no error anywhere. That is the failure worth internalising: everything worked, nothing errored, and the product lied to a customer waiting for a delivery.

</Expect>

## Step 4: a rolling restart under load

The failure from A4.5's Breaks box, tested deliberately: with all three clients connected and ingestion at load, restart the streaming service one instance at a time.

Watch for the thundering herd — every dropped client reconnecting at once. Jittered backoff on the client is the fix, and this is the only way to find out whether yours works.
